The top lane is the ragamroll itself, on its side: time runs left to right as it does for the beads, and a note sits at its own pitch on the raga's own shruti rows, with its gamaka drawn through it. It is the real sound and its timing, and it is not editable here. Below it sit the written swaras and the sahitya, as loose beads waiting to be placed.

Work left to right. The frontier note is ringed in gold. Click a bead to select it, shift-click a later one to take the whole run, then Attach to bind that group to the frontier note. The note fills, those beads lock, and the frontier moves on. Leave empty passes over a note — that is how silence is written, since there is no rest in the notation and an unfilled note simply has nothing sung on it.

A group belongs to exactly one note. Drag the right edge of an attached sahitya bead to show a syllable still sounding past its note, or press Carry – on the note it runs into — one syllable, two notes.

Click the ruler to set A, where Play begins; shift-click for B, where it turns back to A. Play pauses where it stands and resumes from there.

Click a lane's name to work on it — Leave empty and Carry say which lane they will act on. Long stretches with no sahitya are ordinary: shift-click the last note of the stretch in the roll and the frontier steps over all of it, and what was left empty stays drawn as a thin bar under those notes.

Where a stretch runs note for note, ctrl-click the last bead of the run — or the last note of it in the roll — and the whole run is bound at once, one bead per note. alt-click takes work back: on a bead, that lane from its note on; on a note, both lanes from there on.

To take work back in the middle of a piece — the case a half-finished file always turns into — click the note in the roll and press Detach from here. It releases that note's groups and every group placed after them, and rewinds the frontier to it.
